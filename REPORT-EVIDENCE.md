# What the drawing process can tell you

Recorded timestamps can describe the order of labelled elements, the time spent with the pen down on each subject and returns to a subject. A greater person share means more retained active drawing time was labelled Person; it does not establish self-focus, anxiety, coping capacity or a personality trait. A first-to-last timeline span includes gaps and other subjects; it is not active time. Label quality, complexity, speed, hardware, instruction and artistic decisions affect these patterns. Fills have no recorded duration, erased/undone history is absent, and unlabelled retained strokes stay in the denominator.

These observations are useful conversation starting points: what were you trying to express in the part you worked on most, what changed on a return, and what would you choose to add? Suggested next steps are optional choices, not prescribed treatment. The user's actual feelings/available-support selections and if/when–then plan are stronger grounds for personal action than a geometric drawing score.

PITR can be used as a metaphor in facilitated dialogue, but the cited summary explicitly calls for more empirical research. This app's time-share charts are software measurements, not a validated PITR scoring instrument. [1] A human-figure study found poor single-feature discrimination; it is not a direct validation study of this app or PITR. [2] WHO's illustrated stress guide offers evidence-informed practical skills; adapting small-step prompts does not make the custom app a clinically validated treatment. [3]

The PDF uses a disciplined clinical-editorial design for readability but is titled Personal reflection report. It contains no diagnosis, medical certification, normative percentile or inferred stress/coping score. The evidence labels separate recorded data, artist-described scene details and user-selected feelings/actions. Original—not AI-restyled—pixels are used for the artwork evidence. Incomplete descriptions are explicit.
