# Practical reflection activities with visible outcomes

The new activities turn choices into a personal summary rather than asking the user to fill three disconnected text boxes. Naming selected feelings becomes a word cloud whose emphasis is set by the user. Matching feelings to colours produces explicit statements of that person's associations. Choosing supports and an observable action produces a usable when/then plan.

WHO's *Doing What Matters in Times of Stress* offers practical self-help skills for coping with adversity and describes its guide as informed by evidence and extensive field testing.[1] This supports using small, understandable activities rather than elaborate interpretation scores. It does **not** validate this custom app or its word-cloud interface as treatment.

NHS Every Mind Matters explains that thoughts, feelings and actions are connected and provides practical self-help techniques and a personalised action-plan resource.[2] The app uses that broad choice-to-action principle, but does not reproduce the NHS quiz or claim to provide CBT.

Colour matching is deliberately personal. Jonauskaite and colleagues found patterns in colour-emotion associations that were further shaped by linguistic and geographic proximity.[3] Population patterns do not identify an individual's personality or feelings from their pigment choices. Therefore the output says which colour the user chose for a feeling, not what that colour supposedly proves about them.

The existing nine PITR references, disclaimer and support information remain separate and unchanged. These new activities are voluntary reflection tools, not diagnostic tests. No drawing upload, paid API or generative inference is needed to complete or export them.
